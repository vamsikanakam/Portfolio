01 / The flagship program

Program leadership made a complex launch executable.

I led the Optical Bulk Electrochemistry Liquid TEM Holder program from executive charter to commercial release. I translated Voice of Customer (VoC) into program drivers, structured work packages and ownership, and coordinated cross-functional dependencies across Engineering, Scientific Applications, Manufacturing, Quality and Commercial teams. Engineering retained ownership of technical design; my role was to make the work executable as one program.

03 / Building the delivery system

Build a shared PMO rhythm for visible ownership, risks and delivery.

Across Engineering, Sales and Leadership, I built a shared execution rhythm so teams could spend less time chasing updates and more time moving decisions forward. Standard status reporting, PMO dashboards and KPIs, risk registers, stakeholder coordination, schedule and budget control, financial governance, project-performance tracking (including SPI), and executive reporting helped make delivery health and next actions easier to see.

Across 17 cross-functional stakeholders, redesigned tracking reduced status follow-ups by:

Established 12 standardized PM operating procedures across Engineering, Sales and Leadership within two months, enabling repeatable planning, tracking and escalation.

04 / Executive visibility and leadership support

Turn delivery information into leadership decisions.

I consolidated PMIS information into executive reporting on program health, capacity, resource constraints, risks, priorities and performance, including updates for senior management and the CEO. Program health reports and Time-0 action trackers made blockers, commitments, owners and follow-through visible. This leadership-support work connected daily PMO activity with schedule and budget control, financial governance, project-performance indicators and the decisions needed to keep work moving.

This extended the PMO role beyond collecting status. The purpose was to connect delivery information to decisions: what is blocked, where resources are constrained, what needs attention, and which actions have to move next.

05 / Business capacity and production planning

Translate long-range demand into production capacity.

I worked with executive leadership to redesign operational planning around capacity forecasts and resource requirements. A five-year planning framework connected longer-range business demand to production capacity and throughput planning.

To translate the long-range view into operational choices, I owned rolling 12-month capacity and production planning, converting the five-year sales plan into resource and scheduling priorities. Quarterly baseline updates and ongoing tracking helped surface collisions earlier.
